Fire early-warning: detect the fire before the smoke alarm

Saffire VI showed that smoke reached the alarm threshold only after most of the toxic gas had already been released. This detector watches CO, CO₂ and smoke together, learns the cabin's normal baseline, and raises an alarm as soon as the sensors change together in a fire-like way.

Columns: time_s, co2_ppm, co_ppm, smoke_mg_m3 (any two or more sensors). One row per reading.
How it works. 1) Learn each sensor's mean and noise from the first seconds (unsupervised; no fire labels needed). 2) Track each sensor's standardized upward drift with robust CUSUM change detection (clipped scores, 3-reading persistence). 3) Fuse by combustion physics: alarm when CO rises together with CO₂ or smoke. CO₂ alone (breathing) or a smoke blip alone (dust) doesn't trigger it.

Where has NASA actually tested?

Each box is the oxygen and pressure range an investigation covered (dots are single reported test points). Diamonds are crew environments. Anything outside the shaded boxes has limited or no direct microgravity evidence.

Only investigations whose sources report both ends of their oxygen and pressure ranges are drawn. FLEX (up to 40% O₂, up to 3 atm) is left off because its lower bounds aren't reported. SoFIE is dashed because it shows the planned design envelope, not confirmed test points. A 100% O₂ spacesuit atmosphere sits far above the top edge and has no direct coverage here.

How it works

The ranking engine

Every investigation gets a score out of 100 from five transparent parts:

  • Condition coverage (40): whether the target oxygen, pressure and ventilation fall inside the tested range, decaying with distance outside it. Unreported ranges earn partial credit.
  • Fuel match (15): overlap between the selected material types and those tested.
  • Hazard match (20): overlap with the selected hazards (ignition, spread, smoke, suppression…).
  • Fire-safety relevance (15): how directly the investigation addresses crew fire safety.
  • Keywords (+25): BM25 text relevance to any keywords you type; when used, the total is rescaled back to 100.

The safety brief is generated by rules over the same data: it flags conditions outside every tested envelope and points to the closest evidence. No black box: every score shows why.

Data and honesty

The dataset is hand-curated from NASA publications (the ISS combustion Researcher's Guide, NTRS papers on Saffire, BASS-II and ACME, and NASA mission pages). Values a source does not report are left blank rather than guessed.

This is a research and education tool, not an operational safety system. Use it to find and compare evidence, then go to the cited NASA source.
